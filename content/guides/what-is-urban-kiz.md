# What is urban kiz?

Urban kiz is a partner dance that grew out of kizomba in Europe in the early
2010s. It is danced in a close hold, mostly in straight lines and with a lot of
stops, isolations and play with the music, to a mix of kizomba-derived and
urban tracks: ghetto zouk, tarraxo, R&B edits, and whatever a DJ decides fits.

If you have seen a video of two people dancing slowly and precisely in what
looks like a corridor, pausing on the beat and moving one body part at a time,
that was probably urban kiz.

## How it differs from kizomba

Kizomba is an Angolan dance with its own music, history and community, and the
two are related but not the same. Where kizomba is circular, grounded and
travels with the music, urban kiz is more linear, more stop-and-go, and more
about musical interpretation than about the walk. The music is different too:
a kizomba set and an urban kiz set can be an hour apart in tempo and feel.

Most festivals run separate rooms or separate slots for each, and most
teachers will tell you which one they teach. On this site, "urban kiz" means
urban kiz events, or events with an urban kiz room, and not every kizomba
event in Europe.

## Where it happens

**Socials** are the regular nights: a weekly or monthly party in a bar or a
studio where people dance for a few hours. No stage, no classes, sometimes a
short taster at the start. They are the heart of a city's scene and the first
thing to look up when you travel. The [socials page](/socials) lists them by
city, with the date somebody last confirmed each one is still running.

**Workshops** are classes with a visiting teacher, usually a weekend, usually
followed by a party.

**Weekenders and festivals** are two to four days of workshops by day and
parties by night, with teachers and DJs from across Europe. A weekender is the
smaller version. Festivals are where you meet the rest of the scene, and where
most people travel for. The [calendar](/events) lists what is coming up by
month and country.

## Going for the first time

You do not need a partner. Socials are open floors: people ask each other to
dance, dance one or two songs, and thank each other. Arriving alone is the
normal case, and being new is not a problem as long as you say so.

A beginner class before the social, where there is one, is the fastest way in.
Failing that, watch for a while, then ask someone who looks relaxed rather than
someone who looks impressive.

Wear whatever you can move in. Shoes with a smooth sole turn better than
trainers.

## Finding it near you

Pick your city on the [cities page](/cities). If it is not there, that means
nobody has listed it yet, not that nothing is happening: ask in the local
kizomba groups, and when you find the social, [add it](/submit) so the next
person does not have to ask.
